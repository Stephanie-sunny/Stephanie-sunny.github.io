Building Sustainable Cities for Malaysia's Future

Explore how Malaysia faces severe urban challenges—from vulnerable wooden structures to intense heat islands and car-dominated roads—and learn how eco-engineering reshapes our communities.

Part 1

The Urban Problem in Malaysia

Aging housing infrastructure, severe heat island effects, and unsustainably high traffic density create risks for citizens.

Aging Wooden Houses

Traditional aging wooden homes (*Rumah Papan*) and poorly maintained squatter structures face fire hazards, lack insulation, and rot under heavy tropical monsoon rains.

Car-Centric Highways

Malaysian cities have very high private car density. Without safe, connected pedestrian walkways or dedicated lanes, pedestrians face dangerous conditions daily.

Urban Heat & Flooding

Excessive asphalt surfaces trap solar heat while lacking permeable drainage systems, frequently triggering sudden *banjir kilat* (flash floods) in towns.

Phase 1: Eco-Foundation & Rain Storage

Transit-Oriented Development (TOD)

Designing high-density residential hubs around MRT and LRT stations in Klang Valley, Penang, and JB reduces highway traffic congestion.

Urban Tree Canopy Expansion

Planting shade-giving native trees along roadways counters the Urban Heat Island effect and cools down dense city neighborhoods.
