The problem in Malaysia

Old and poorly maintained houses, rising temperatures in cities, and heavy traffic are making everyday life more difficult and less safe for people.

Old wooden houses

Traditional old wooden homes (such as "Rumah Papan") and poorly maintained houses face many fire hazards, lack insulation and can easily become damaged or start to rot during Malaysia’s heavy monsoon rains.

Roads mainly built for cars

Many Malaysian cities have too many cars on the road, making traffic heavy and travelling difficult. With limited safe walkways and cycling lanes, people who walk or cycle often struggle to get around safely.

Hotter cities and frequent flooding

Large areas of roads and concrete absorb and trap heat, making cities feel hotter. When heavy rain falls, the water cannot drain away properly which often cause flash floods (such as "Banjir kilat") in towns.

Extra Solutions

Building communities around public transport

Building more homes and apartments near MRT and LRT stations in places like Klang Valley and Penang makes it easier for people to use public transport and helps reduce traffic jams.

Planting more trees in cities

Planting more local trees along roads provides shade, reduces the heat, and makes city areas cooler and more comfortable for everyone.
